A New Vision. A New Chapter. A Brighter Future.

August 2026·Official Statement

AIS sets out a new management vision focused on excellence, character, a stronger school environment and a brighter future for its students.

Official AIS NewsAugust 2026School Transformation
Bustan-e-Ahmad, Accra · August 2026

T. I. Ahmadiyya International School is beginning a new chapter under its new management, with a vision centred on academic excellence, discipline, high moral values, integrity and service to humanity.

The new management has welcomed parents, students, teachers, staff and other stakeholders to what it describes as a new and exciting chapter for AIS. The direction outlined in the August statement is ambitious: to build a modern, safe and highly respected school while strengthening the academic, moral and service-oriented foundations of the institution.

That vision is being accompanied by changes to the school environment and a programme of improvements intended to shape the experience of students and staff. The management has identified five priorities as the standards it wants AIS to be known for, placing the development of children at the centre of the next phase.

Five priorities shape the new direction

At the centre of the new direction are five priorities: Academic Excellence, Discipline, High Moral Values, Integrity and Service to Humanity. Together, they provide the framework through which the new management says it wants AIS to develop academically, socially and morally.

  • Academic Excellence — strengthening the quality and opportunities of learning.
  • Discipline — building a safe, orderly and purposeful school culture.
  • High Moral Values — keeping character and values central to education.
  • Integrity — promoting responsibility, trust and high standards.
  • Service to Humanity — preparing children to contribute positively to society.

Transformation has already begun

The transformation is not being presented as a future-only ambition. According to the August statement, work is already under way to clear, clean and beautify the school environment. The programme extends into classrooms, boarding and washroom facilities, ICT and learning resources, security, sports and recreation, and school transportation.

Taken together, the areas identified by management point to an effort to improve both the day-to-day school environment and the resources available to students and staff.

Investment in teachers and the school identity

People are also part of the planned change. The new management says it is recruiting qualified and dedicated teachers, while AIS is being rebranded to reflect the new standards, opportunities and quality of education it intends to provide.

Putting students at the centre

The statement identifies the school's 86 current students and their parents as being at the heart of the transformation. Its stated goal is to provide every child with a safe, conducive and inspiring environment in which to learn, grow and succeed.

Parents, students, teachers, staff and other stakeholders are being asked to support the process with trust and cooperation as the school moves into this next phase.

The message from the new management is clear: the transition is intended not simply to change administration, but to build a stronger AIS and a brighter future for its children.
